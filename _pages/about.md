I'm Harry Rogers, a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering (IBME), University of Oxford. I'm a member of Prof. Alison Noble's [Biomedical Image Analysis group](https://eng.ox.ac.uk/biomedia/) and the [SHAIPE](https://www.shaipe.co.uk/people.html) consortium — a collaborative group spanning engineering, psychology, and clinical practice.

My core research thread is **eXplainable AI (XAI) and human–AI collaboration** in computer vision. I'm interested in the mechanics of how AI systems and people share decisions, evidence, and trust — not as a wrapper around an opaque model, but as a property of the system from the start.

At Oxford I'm working on **AI as an instructor** within SHAIPE — using vision-language models in human–AI collaboration for skill instruction and learning. A recent thread of that work is *[Learning to Defer: A Survey](/publication/2025-l2d-survey)* (Strong, Sun, **Rogers**, Higham, Noble, 2025), which maps the literature on systems that choose between predicting autonomously and deferring to a human expert.

I completed my DPhil at the [AgriFoRwArdS CDT](https://agriforwards-cdt.blogs.lincoln.ac.uk/) (University of East Anglia, 2021–2024), supervised by Prof. Beatriz De La Iglesia in collaboration with Syngenta. The thesis worked on XAI for agricultural computer vision — evaluating precision spraying systems, identifying spray deposits, and building interpretable quantised CNNs that run on resource-constrained agri-robots (KDIR 2023 Best Paper Award; TAROS 2023 Best Application nomination). The medical-imaging work I did between PhD and Oxford — automating the Clock Drawing Test with saliency maps (EPIA 2024) and quality assurance for home spirometry (IEEE ISCC 2025) — was an XAI thread first, applied to clinical data. Before all of that I read for an MSc in Robotics & Autonomous Systems at the University of Lincoln and a BSc in Computer Science also at Lincoln.
