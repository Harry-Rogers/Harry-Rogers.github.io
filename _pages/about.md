I'm Harry Rogers, a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering (IBME), University of Oxford, working in Prof. Alison Noble's Biomedical Image Analysis group.

My broad research interest is **human-AI collaboration in medical AI** — how clinicians, learners, and AI systems work together when the stakes are real. I'm currently focused on AI in medical education: the imaging-vs-education tension dissolves once both are treated as instances of the same underlying question about how humans and models should share decisions, evidence, and trust.

Before joining the Noble group I worked on medical-imaging AI applications, including automating the Clock Drawing Test with deep learning and saliency maps (EPIA 2024) and quality assurance for home spirometry using machine learning (IEEE ISCC 2025). Both projects centred on making model behaviour legible to clinicians rather than treating AI as a black box. <!-- TODO(owner): refine -->

I completed my DPhil at the [AgriFoRwArdS CDT](https://agriforwards-cdt.blogs.lincoln.ac.uk/) (University of East Anglia), supervised by Prof. Beatriz De La Iglesia in collaboration with Syngenta, with the thesis *Closing The Loop on Precision Spraying* — covering sprayer evaluation, deposit identification, and interpretable quantized CNNs for in-field agricultural robotics. Before that I completed an MSc in Robotics & Autonomous Systems at UEA and a BSc in Computer Science at the University of Lincoln. <!-- TODO(owner): refine -->
