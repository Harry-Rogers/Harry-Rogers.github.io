Presented at *Drink Outside the Box*, a public-engagement event series that brings researchers into informal venues to talk about their work with a general audience over a drink. My session covered agricultural robotics and precision spraying — what the problem actually is (using less herbicide, more accurately, on real farms), how computer vision and embedded deep learning fit in, and where the technology is honest about its current limits.

The format demanded a different register from a conference talk: no jargon shields, no peer-review safety net, and questions that range from "what does the robot look like" to "is this going to put farmers out of work." Preparing for it meant stripping a year of PhD work down to the few ideas that survive contact with a non-specialist audience, and being explicit about what is research, what is product, and what is still aspirational.

This is part of a wider pattern of outreach work alongside the [Lego Mindstorms school visits](/teaching/2023-lego-mindstorms-outreach) and CDT [organisation committee roles](/teaching/2024-agriforwards-committee).
