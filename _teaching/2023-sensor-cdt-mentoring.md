Mentored teams of first-year PhD students during the UEA Sensor CDT annual *Team Challenge* — a short, intensive group project in which incoming cohort members tackle a real sensing problem end-to-end, from problem framing through prototyping to a public presentation.

My role was to act as a near-peer mentor: close enough to the cohort experience to be useful on day-to-day questions (how to scope a one-week project, where to push back on a brief, when to stop iterating on a sensor rig and start writing the talk), but with enough distance from the assessment to be a safe sounding board. I helped teams pressure-test their experimental designs, gave feedback on draft slides and figures, and shared lessons from my own PhD work in precision agriculture about avoiding the classic "we built it, but we never measured it properly" failure mode.

The Team Challenge sits inside the broader AgriFoRwArdS CDT training programme; mentoring it was a natural complement to the [AgriFoRwArdS organisation work](/teaching/2024-agriforwards-committee).
