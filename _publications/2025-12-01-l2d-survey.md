## Abstract

Learning to Defer (L2D) equips an AI model with the ability to either make an autonomous prediction or defer its decision to an external human expert — a fail-safe that redirects challenging or uncertain cases to where they can be handled more reliably. L2D is a prominent emerging area within human–AI collaboration (HAIC). This survey consolidates the fast-growing L2D literature through a four-branch taxonomy: methodological frameworks (prediction-rejection vs. score-based); optimisation and theory (surrogate loss design, consistency guarantees, hierarchies of theoretical guarantees); task generalisations; and real-world adaptations.
